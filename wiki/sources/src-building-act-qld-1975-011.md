# Queensland Building Act 1975 — Legislative Overview

## Summary

The Building Act 1975 (Qld) is the primary Queensland legislation governing building development approvals, building work, building classification, the licensing and regulation of building certifiers and pool safety inspectors, swimming pool safety, and sustainable building matters. Current as at 1 February 2024, the Act establishes the framework within which the Building Code of Australia (National Construction Code) and the Queensland Development Code apply to building work in Queensland. It binds all persons, including the State, and operates alongside the Planning Act and the QBCC Act.

## Key claims

- The Act defines **building work** to include building, repairing, altering, underpinning, moving, demolishing, excavating or filling for or incidental to those activities, and other work regulated under the building assessment provisions (s 5).
- The **Building Code of Australia (BCA)** is defined in the Act as the *National Construction Code* (volumes 1 and 2, including Queensland Appendixes) published by the Australian Building Codes Board (s 12).
- The **Queensland Development Code (QDC)** consists of such parts as are prescribed by regulation, and the chief executive must publish it on the department's website (s 13).
- Building work complies with the BCA or QDC only if it complies with all relevant performance requirements, achieved either by deemed-to-satisfy/acceptable solution provisions, a performance solution, or a combination of both (s 14).
- **Building certifiers** must be licensed under Chapter 6, Part 3; performing building certifying functions without a licence is prohibited (ss 8, 125).
- Two classes of **private certifier** exist: Class A (holding development approval endorsement) and Class B (without development approval endorsement) (s 9).
- Building certifiers have a **duty to act in the public interest** when performing building certifying functions; performing such functions where a conflict of interest exists is prohibited (ss 127–128).
- **Chapter 7** addresses fire safety requirements for budget accommodation buildings, including conformity with a prescribed fire safety standard and fire safety management plans (ss 217, 226).
- **Chapter 7A** addresses fire safety for residential care buildings built, approved, or applied for before 1 June 2007 (s 231AA).
- **Chapter 8** regulates swimming pool safety, including compliance with the pool safety standard, licensing of pool safety inspectors, regulated pool registers, pool safety certificates, and obligations on sale or letting of premises with regulated pools.
- **Chapter 8A** limits the effect of prohibitions or restrictions (including those in body corporate rules or local laws) that would prevent particular sustainable housing measures for Class 1a, 2, or 10a buildings, including restrictions on roof colours and windows (ss 246O–246S).
- **Chapter 8B** provides for the designation of land as a transport noise corridor for building assessment purposes (ss 246V–246ZA).
- A **demerit points** system applies to building certifiers; accumulation of **30 demerit points** triggers a disqualification procedure (ss 214E, 214I).
- Building development approvals may lapse, and private certifier (Class A) is restricted from extending the currency period under the Planning Act more than once (s 97).

## Caveats

- The document is the table of contents and introductory provisions only; the full operative text of most sections is not included in the upload, limiting the ability to quote specific clause detail beyond what is summarised in the preliminary chapters.
- Current as at 1 February 2024; subsequent amendments to the Act or subordinate instruments (QDC, pool safety standard, fire safety standard) may alter operative requirements.
- This is Queensland-specific legislation; it does not apply in other Australian states or territories.
